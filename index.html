    Raster tools cheat sheet

    Reclassify: turn a raster into a yes/no (1/0) layer, e.g. slope > 20° becomes 1, everything else 0.

    Proximity (buffer): find every cell within (or farther than) some distance of the selected cells. Distances are measured between cell centers, so the next cell over is 5 m away and the diagonal neighbor is 7.1 m away.

    Overlay, Multiply: multiply your 1/0 layers together. A cell only stays 1 if it is 1 in every layer (a logical AND).

    Overlay, Sum: add your 1/0 layers. Each cell's value is the number of criteria it meets, which gives a likelihood score rather than a yes/no answer.

    🌲 There's a sasquatch in these woods

    Hikers keep reporting a large, hairy, surprisingly polite creature in this patch of forest. Your job is to find exactly where it lives.

    You have five raster datasets (elevation, slope, land cover, streams, and hiking trails) and a field guide of sasquatch habits. Turn each habit into a 1/0 criterion layer, then overlay them to narrow 576 cells down to one.

    1. Explore the five rasters. Hover over cells to see their values.
    2. Predict which cell the sasquatch lives in, before you build a model.
    3. Reclassify each raster (and buffer it when needed) to match a field-guide clue.
    4. Overlay your criterion layers.
    5. Search the cell your model points to. You only get 3 searches!

    Tip: some clues need two steps, for example first selecting rock outcrops and then finding cells within 10 m of them.

    🥾 Out of trail mix

    Your search party has gone home hungry, and the sasquatch is still out there. Look at the clues your search party sent back, check your criterion layers against the field guide, and try again.
